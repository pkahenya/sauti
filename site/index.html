Kenya’s languages, recorded by Kenyans, open to everyone.

Sauti pays people to record and check short voice clips in Sheng, Kikuyu, Dholuo, Luhya, Kalenjin, Somali and Kenyan English. Each validated clip earns two US cents in USDC, with its consent and origin recorded on Solana. The finished dataset is free for anyone to use.

Sheng

Nairobi’s street language, built mostly on Swahili and English and reinvented by every generation.

Kenya talks in layers. Speech technology hears very little of it.

A matatu conductor, a Kisumu fish trader and a student’s voice note can move between Sheng, a mother tongue, Swahili and English in one sentence. Voice tools learn from recorded speech, and for most Kenyan languages the open recordings are few and mostly scripted. Sheng and everyday code-switching barely appear at all.

So call centre bots, voice search and transcription tools fail the people who would use them most. Swahili already has sizeable open collections, so Sauti starts with the languages and ways of speaking that have the least.

The people to fix this are already here. Kenya has one of Africa’s largest workforces in AI data work, much of it labelling data for products built elsewhere. Sauti points that skill at an open resource Kenya can build on.

How a clip becomes data

  1. 1

    Sign up with a phone number

    We ask for a phone number and the languages you speak, nothing else. A wallet is created in the browser, and prompts never ask for personal details.

  2. 2

    Record or transcribe

    Read short prompts aloud in your language, or type what you hear in someone else’s clip. Each clip lasts a few seconds.

  3. 3

    Three speakers check it

    A clip counts once three other native speakers confirm it is clear and matches its text. Disputed clips go to a language lead.

  4. 4

    Get paid every week

    A validated clip earns two US cents in USDC, and checking clips earns too. Contributors claim their earnings each week to their own wallet.

  5. 5

    Released to everyone

    Validated clips join regular open releases on Hugging Face, each with a datasheet, a licence and a benchmark. The first release lands by month six.

Built on Solana, for the public good

The Solana Foundation funds projects against four tests. This is how Sauti meets each one.

Public good

The dataset, the code and every benchmark are free to use. Start-ups, universities, call centres, hospitals and county governments can build voice tools for Kenyans without paying for data.

Open source

The app, the Solana program and the validation tools are published on GitHub from the first month under an open licence, with a kit for other countries to copy.

Only possible on Solana

A validated clip pays two US cents. Mobile money fees make payments that small uneconomic to send one at a time. On Solana, recording a clip and paying for it costs a fraction of a cent.

When a company licenses a curated dataset, the program splits the fee among every contributor whose clips it contains. The consent and fingerprint log is public: anyone can check it and nobody can quietly rewrite it.

Languages also cross borders where mobile money mostly does not, so Somali and Dholuo speakers in neighbouring countries are paid the same way.

Clear use of funds

After the grant

The open dataset and all the code stay free. Keeyo Digital Solutions keeps Sauti running by licensing curated, domain-specific datasets, such as call centre, fintech and agriculture speech, to AI labs and outsourcing firms.

Every licence fee is split on-chain to the contributors whose clips it contains, so the people who made the data keep earning from it.

Safeguards

Data protection
Built to Kenya’s Data Protection Act, 2019: registration with the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner, plain-language consent, a clear way to withdraw, and no personal data on-chain.
Fraud
Phone-verified accounts, three independent checks per clip, rate limits, reputation scores, random audits, and clawback of unclaimed rewards on confirmed fraud.
Quality
Native-speaker validators for every language, and a benchmark published with every release, so quality is measured rather than claimed.
Money
Contributors hold their own wallets and cash out through licensed providers. Keeyo never holds contributor funds.
